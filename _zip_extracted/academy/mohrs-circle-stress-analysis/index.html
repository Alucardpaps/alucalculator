Mohr's Circle Stress Analysis (Principal Stresses)

In multi-axial loading, the stress at a point depends on the orientation of the plane you are looking at. Mohr's Circle is a powerful graphical method (and mathematical model) to find the maximum and minimum stresses a material will ever see. Real components rarely see pure axial stress. Bending, torsion, and press fits combine into plane-stress states (σx, σy, τxy). Mohr's circle transforms these components to principal stresses and maximum shear — the values used in ductile failure criteria. The Principal Stress Simulator in this lesson mirrors the analytical kernel in AluCalc Mohr Circle Lab. Uniaxial σ = F/A is not enough when bending and torsion coexist. The Mohr Circle Lab visualizes the same transformation the OS kernel evaluates analytically.

Learning objectives

  1. Transform plane-stress components to find principal stresses σ1, σ2
  2. Compute maximum shear τ_max from Mohr circle geometry
  3. Explain why principal values are invariant while σ(θ) and τ(θ) rotate
  4. State assumptions and validity limits
  5. Apply the governing equation with SI units
  6. Connect the result to a design decision and inspection plan
Engineering tipThe center of the circle always represents the average normal stress at that point.

Calculation pipeline

  1. 1
    Center and radius

    The circle center is the average normal stress; radius comes from shear and normal stress difference.

    C = (σx+σy)/2, R = √[(σx−σy)/2)² + τxy²]
  2. 2
    Principal stresses

    Maximum and minimum normal stresses occur where shear is zero.

    σ1,2 = C ± R
  3. 3
    Interactive rotation

    The academy lab rotates θ in real time — the same transform the OS kernel uses for plane-stress probes.

Governing Formula

σ1,2 = (σx + σy)/2 ± √[((σx - σy)/2)² + τxy²]

Step-by-Step Calculation

  1. 1Determine the initial stress state (σx, σy, τxy).
  2. 2Plot the points (σx, τxy) and (σy, -τxy) on the stress axes.
  3. 3Draw the circle passing through these two points.
  4. 4Locate the center and calculate the radius (R).
  5. 5Identify σ1 (max) and σ2 (min) as the intersections with the horizontal axis.
  6. 6Plot σx, σy, τxy on Mohr circle to read σ1, σ2, and τ_max.
  7. 7Compare τ_max to yield/2 for ductile materials per Tresca criterion.
  8. 8List governing standards and company design rules that apply to this problem.
  9. 9Perform a unit and order-of-magnitude sanity check before finalizing.
  10. 10Document assumptions, inputs, outputs, and residual risks for design review.
  11. 11Identify the manufacturing/inspection implication of the calculated result.

Worked Example

Input Parameters

  • σx = 100 MPa
  • σy = 40 MPa
  • τxy = 40 MPa

Calculation

Center = 70. Radius = √[(30)² + 40²] = 50. → σ1 = 120 MPa, σ2 = 20 MPa.

Why This Matters

  • Ductile materials typically fail due to maximum shear stress (τmax).
  • Brittle materials fail due to maximum principal stress (σ1).
  • Mohr's Circle allows designers to predict the angle of failure.
  • Deep understanding here prevents expensive late-stage redesign and field failures.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing the 2θ angle in Mohr's Circle with the θ angle in the physical world.
  • Incorrectly plotting the sign of the shear stress.
  • Forgetting to check the 3D stress state (Triaxial stress).
  • Copying results without restating assumptions and units.
  • Optimizing for one limit state while ignoring another (e.g. strength vs stiffness vs fatigue).

Technical Q&A

What is a principal stress?

It is a normal stress that acts on a plane where the shear stress is exactly zero.

Are principal stresses unique?

Yes — σ1 and σ2 are invariants; only the orientation of principal planes changes with loading.

What is plane stress?

One principal stress is zero (thin plates, free surfaces) — simplifies 3D problems to 2D.

When is Mohr circle insufficient?

Triaxial stress states require 3D failure criteria (von Mises in full tensor form).

How do I know my assumptions are still valid?

Check regime limits (elastic vs plastic, laminar vs turbulent, thin-wall, small deflection). If any assumption is violated, switch models or escalate to FEA/test.

What belongs in the calculation package?

Inputs with units, sketches/FBDs, equations, numeric steps, allowables, safety policy, revision/effectivity, and linked standards.

How should I use the AluCalc calculator with this lesson?

Reproduce the worked example first, then change one variable at a time to build intuition for sensitivity and design trade-offs.

Key takeaways

  • Principal planes have zero shear — design checks often use σ1
  • τ_max = Mohr circle radius — drives yielding in ductile materials
  • Combined loading requires plane-stress analysis before failure criteria
  • Units and regimes are part of the answer
  • Standards close the loop between theory and shop floor
  • Document for the next engineer — including future you
